Variational wavefunctions for ideal anyon gas: superfluid and skyrmion Hall crystal

Authors: Ya-Hui Zhang

The ground state of an ideal gas of anyons with statistical angle $θ\in(0,π)$ is a long-standing open problem, with renewed interest from the recent observations of fractional Chern insulators. In this work we construct variational wavefunctions that build in the exact anyon statistics and combine theory-guided composite-boson, composite-fermion and parton wavefunctions with neural-network correlations on the sphere. For spinless and SU(2) semions we confirm the paired anyon superfluid, well below the best crystal states. At $θ=π/3$ liquids and crystals are closer in energy for the spinless case. For $θ=π/3$ anyons with SU(3) flavor, we find two candidates. One is a skyrmion Hall crystal, in which the anyons form an integer quantum Hall state in the effective magnetic field from the spin texture. The other is a chiral color superconductor of six-anyon clusters, the color-flavor-locked pairing descendant of a holon metal with SU(3) color gauge symmetry and SU(3) flavor symmetry; its wavefunction is a hyperdeterminant rather than a product of parton determinants. Within our variational ansatz the color superconductor has better energy than both crystals and the color-flavor locked phases with U(1)$^2$ and Z$_3$ gauge groups. For twisted MoTe$_2$ this suggests that the reentrant integer quantum anomalous Hall phase is the skyrmion Hall crystal of the doped anyons, and that the observed superconductivity may be the color superconductor, which can undergo a transition to an $f+if$ BCS superconductor with a jump of the chiral central charge by 3.

Reconstructing hidden observables via quantum snapshot thermometry

Authors: Jie Ren, Andrew Hallam, Luheng Zhao, Huanqian Loh, Zlatko Papić

Projective snapshots are a standard readout tool for quantum simulators, but they directly probe only observables that are diagonal in the measurement basis. We show how local equilibration and an independently calibrated equilibrium equation of state can reveal otherwise hidden conserved-density profiles. We demonstrate numerically that such ``quantum snapshot thermometry'' can recover energy fronts and a diffusion constant, distinguish spin transport from energy spreading, and reveal conserved-charge profiles invisible in the initial measurements. We also identify symmetry-imposed ambiguities in the readout and show how prior knowledge of the spatial structure can resolve them. Fixed-basis snapshot thermometry thus provides quantitative hydrodynamic information beyond transport exponents, bypassing additional measurement bases and quantum state tomography.

Renormalization for Robust Memory without Synchronization

Authors: Shashwat Chakraborty, Jong Yeon Lee

Local error correction can preserve classical information against noise, but protection under synchronized updates need not survive when sites evolve at independent random times. Establishing stability is particularly challenging when protection relies on the collective action of competing local rules. Here we develop a spacetime renormalization framework that reveals how independently acting local updates can collectively stabilize memory. We identify sufficient conditions under which the geometry of stable interfaces and suppression of defect propagation produce reliable erosion at large scales. Applying this framework, we prove stability of the asynchronous \textsf{R} squeezing code and introduce robust processes in two and three dimensions. In these systems, both encoded phases persist under sufficiently weak noise, even when the noise favors one phase. Our results provide a criterion for designing autonomous memories sustained by competing local stochastic dynamics.

Light-Cone Friedel Oscillations: a Window into van Hove Superconductivity

Authors: Archisman Panigrahi, Vladislav Poliakov, Leonid Levitov

Van Hove (vH) singularities can enhance superconductivity by generating a large pairing susceptibility. Yet, it remains challenging to experimentally establish whether pairing really occurs near these points. We show that the saddle-point dispersion near a vH point gives rise to a `light-cone' vH-selective behavior of Friedel oscillations, which is drastically different from the familiar concentric circles. Each vH point maps onto its own real-space light-cone sector, allowing STM around a single impurity to measure whether---and how strongly---that saddle-point quasiparticle sector is gapped. A superconducting gap near vH points suppresses the oscillations in the directions corresponding to peaks in the gap function angular dependence, a behavior that provides a signature of vH-related pairing at STM-accessible length scales. This STM protocol, targeting vH superconductivity, is capable of determining whether superconducting pairing emerges near the vH points or elsewhere on the Fermi surface.

JT gravity as a conformal scaling limit of random flat combinatorial disks

Authors: Morris Ang

We prove that flat Jackiw--Teitelboim (JT) gravity arises as a conformal scaling limit of random flat combinatorial disks. We give a clean criterion, formulated in terms of branching explorations, for a discrete model to converge to this conjecturally universal limit. For simplicity, we apply this criterion to a Boltzmann ensemble of disks with rectilinear boundary, defined by summing over combinatorial types and integrating over lengths. As the length of a distinguished side tends to infinity, the rescaled surface converges in law to a continuum random surface prescribed by Ferrari's path-integral formulation of JT gravity. Convergence is in the topology of smooth convergence of the conformal factors on compact subsets of the interior when uniformizing in a fixed domain; the limiting conformal factor is described by a harmonic Gaussian field closely related to holomorphic multiplicative chaos. The crux is a branching exploration of flat JT gravity whose lengths evolve as conditioned Cauchy processes and whose unexplored components are conditionally independent. We construct this exploration using a conformal welding theorem proved in a companion paper, and show that it is the scaling limit of Budd's row-by-row exploration of the discrete disks, analogous to the peeling of random planar maps. Under a tightness condition, our criterion upgrades this convergence to a conformal scaling limit for the discrete disks. Other natural combinatorial disk models admit analogous first-row recursions, providing a route to their scaling limits.

Comparison of entangling strategies for unitary synthesis

Authors: Jonas Wunder, Hendrik Weimer, Ricardo Costa de Almeida

Trapped-ion quantum computers are promising as a platform for realizing quantum computational advantage. They promise long coherence times, near all-to-all connectivity, and potential multiqubit gates as distinguishing advantages compared to competing platforms. Regarding these advantages, it is crucial to understand the trade-offs between increasing engineering complexity and actual improvements in running quantum algorithms with a more flexible gate set. In this work, we investigate this question and develop a versatile toolset to evaluate the performance of gate sets composed of native Mølmer--Sørensen multiqubit gates. To this end, we employ unitary synthesis as an application-agnostic benchmark target and identify specific hardware requirements for multiqubit gates to be advantageous.

From extreme weather to agricultural consequences with agentic AI

Authors: Minghan Yu, Mengyu Dai, Yixin Wen, Haizhao Yang

Extreme weather is forecast days ahead, yet its consequences for agriculture remain hard to predict: a forecast says where a storm will strike, not which crops will suffer or how much is at risk, and the records that could tell span many sources. We present WxCrop, the first agent that judges agricultural hazards from physically interpretable weather indices examined by causal analysis, and the first that combines weather and agricultural information to predict crop loss reliably for past and forecast dates. We demonstrate it in Florida on hurricanes, freezes and dry heat, with a loss model calibrated on insurance records for each, and the same procedure extends to other hazards and areas. Three checks show reliability: the hurricane index detects recorded cyclone days with an AUC of 0.98, recorded losses in held-out years are reproduced to within 2%, and independent production records confirm the county rankings (rank correlation 0.63). Users ask the agent for each crop group's loss as a fraction of expected season value, every number sourced, so that governments, growers and scientists respond to agricultural disasters quickly.

SonoWorld4D: From One Video to a 4D Audio-Visual Scene via Diffusion Synchronization

Authors: Derong Jin, Xiyi Chen, Ming C. Lin, Ruohan Gao

Recent progress in visual scene generation has extended beyond static 3D scenes toward the more challenging problem of generating dynamic 4D scenes from minimal input. However, sound, despite being intrinsically coupled with dynamic objects and events, remains largely unmodeled in prior work. We introduce Video-to-4DAVScene, a new task that turns a video into an explorable, time-varying 4D audio-visual scene, and present the SonoWorld4D framework for this task. Given a monocular video, our method generates a 360 static background, reconstructs explicit motion trajectories of dynamic, sound-emitting foreground objects, and synthesizes a physically-grounded sound field consistent across multiple sources through dual-space audio diffusion synchronization. The resulting 4D scene supports real-time novel-view visual and spatial audio rendering. Evaluation on real-world dynamic scenes and a user study demonstrates SonoWorld4D generates 4D audio-visual scenes with perceptually realistic, physically grounded spatial audio consistent with scene geometry and visual content.

Competitive scaling of small-subsystem-optimization heuristics for LABS

Authors: Ginger E. Lau, Adil A. Gangat

The low autocorrelation binary sequences (LABS) problem, often formulated as a spin-lattice energy-minimization problem, is a combinatorial optimization problem with a very rugged energy landscape. We demonstrate that iterative optimization of very small subsystems of n spins that are decoupled from the rest of the system achieves global optimization of LABS with a time-to-solution scaling that is competitive with heuristics that optimize the global system of N spins directly. Our isolated-subsystem heuristic with n = 4 achieves a time scaling of O(1.33^N ) on the interval N = [27, 60], while the current state-of-the-art classical and quantum scalings are respectively O(1.34^N ) and O(1.21^N ). Our results suggest that specialized optimization hardware with only small numbers of logical spins may be of practical relevance, and that quantum speedups demonstrated in the absence of decomposition may not provide a practical quantum advantage for large-scale problems that require decomposition.

Entanglement diagnostics of stochastic-resetting steady states: theory and measurement on superconducting qubits

Authors: Jakob Murauer, Sabine Tornow, Manas Kulkarni, Eli Barkai, Satya N. Majumdar, Monalisa Singh Roy

Stochastic resetting drives quantum systems into nonequilibrium stationary states unreachable under uninterrupted unitary evolution, and their entanglement depends on whether the reset history is resolved or averaged over. We study this interplay in the transverse-field Ising chain, deriving closed-form results for two qubits and evaluating chains of up to 12 qubits exactly. In the two-qubit system, these closed-form results show that phase matching between energy eigenstates produces resonances, and their visibility in the stationary state is determined by the symmetry of the reset state. Measurements on an IBM superconducting processor show the entropies evaluated before and after the reset average closely following noiseless predictions with no fitted parameter, while concurrence lies below prediction across most of the hardware measurements. The two diagnostics respond differently because they read different components of the same reconstructed state, and a depolarizing model captures the concurrence suppression with one fitted parameter. The difference between the two entropies, i. e., the Jensen gap of entanglement entropy, equals the Holevo information that a subsystem retains about the time since the last reset. With a Bell-state reset, this information grows with chain length across a half-chain cut over the sizes studied, without visible saturation at the smaller reset probabilities. Since reset times are sampled and recorded classically, the trajectory-averaged entropy, and with it the Jensen gap, is obtained without postselection, in contrast to monitored circuits, where trajectory-resolved entanglement requires sampling exponentially rare measurement records.
